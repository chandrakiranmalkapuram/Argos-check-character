# Voice Confusion Engine Specification & Documentation

The **Voice Confusion Engine** is an independent, deterministic algorithmic module that identifies and ranks confusable warehouse location codes for voice-directed picking systems.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy & The Same-Check Gate (Invariant I2)

In high-volume voice picking environments (such as Argos and Sainsbury's distribution centres), operatives use headsets to receive pick instructions and must verbally confirm shelf locations by reciting a two-digit or single-letter **check character** printed on the shelf label. The headset software evaluates the spoken check character against an internal verification algorithm.

- **Different-check codes are self-correcting**: If an operative mishears or mispronounces a location whose true check character differs from the intended location, the headset voice recognition software immediately fails the verification check. The picker cannot confirm the pick and is prompted to repeat or re-read the location.
- **Same-check collisions are critical operational hazards**: If two distinct location codes produce the **exact same check character**, an operative who goes to or recites the wrong location will have their check character silently accepted by the headset. The incorrect item is picked, inventory counts diverge, and error detection is delayed until store dispatch or customer receipt.

### The Same-Check Invariant (I2)
> **Every candidate suggested in Tier 1 (Reported), Tier 2 (Model-Similar), Tier 3 (Possible), and Section (d) (Arithmetic Pool) MUST share the identical check character of the searched location.**

Any community report where the reported code has a *different* check character is strictly partitioned into **Section (e)** (*"Reported confusions with a different check character: the headset would normally reject these"*). It is never permitted into the ranked candidate tiers.

---

## 2. Candidate Tiers and Classification Rules

Candidate locations are generated by scanning valid location codes in the warehouse grammar and filtered against the same-check gate. Candidates are classified into deterministic tiers:

| Tier | Name | Criteria | Cap / Bounds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1** | **Reported & Verified** | Verified community or moderator report sharing the same check character, with an acoustic reason (`similar_sounding` or `headset_misrecognition`) and status `approved`/`confirmed` (or provenance `moderator_confirmed` with `community_submitted`). | Elevated above algorithmic ranking. |
| **Tier 2** | **Model-Similar** | Algorithmic acoustic alignment distance $\le 0.55$, differing by at most **2 tokens** (`MAX_DIFFERING_TOKENS_TIER2 = 2`), with **0 far tokens** (all differing tokens must have pairwise acoustic distance $\le 0.55$). | Differing tokens $\le 2$, far tokens $= 0$, mean dist $\le 0.55$. |
| **Tier 3** | **Possible Sound-Alikes** | Algorithmic acoustic alignment differing by at most **2 tokens** (`MAX_DIFFERING_TOKENS_TIER3 = 2`), with at most **1 far token** (distance $\le 0.90$), and mean acoustic distance $\le 0.90$. | Differing tokens $\le 2$, far tokens $\le 1$, mean dist $\le 0.90$. |
| **Pool** | **All Same-Check Codes (Arithmetic Pool)** | All valid locations in the same-check modulo 26 partition that differ acoustically by 2 or more far tokens. | Computed lazily upon disclosure toggle. |

---

## 3. Near Threshold Convention (`MAX_NEAR_ACOUSTIC_DIST = 0.55`)

Spoken tokens are compared pairwise using an articulatory phonetic feature comparator based on place, manner, voicing, and vowel height/backness.

- **Acoustic Distance $= 0.0$ (`identical`)**: Exact phonetic match (e.g., `ALPHA` vs `ALPHA`).
- **Acoustic Distance $\le 0.55$ (`close`)**: Acoustically near pairs that share articulatory manner and place, frequently confused in noisy warehouse acoustics (e.g., `M` / `N` = 0.175, `B` / `P` = 0.225, `D` / `T` = 0.225, `F` / `S` = 0.350).
- **Acoustic Distance $> 0.55$ (`far`)**: Distinct phonetic tokens (e.g., `A` / `Z` = 0.875, `0` / `9` = 0.950).
- **Token Gap Cost $= 0.90$**: Penalty applied in Needleman-Wunsch gapped alignments when aligning unequal token sequences.

---

## 4. Pronunciation Lexicon Assumptions

1. **NATO Phonetic Alphabet**: Letters are spoken using NATO standard words:
   `A` = ALPHA, `B` = BRAVO, `C` = CHARLIE, ..., `Z` = ZULU.
2. **Digit-by-Digit Vocalisation**: Numbers are vocalised digit-by-digit:
   - Aisle `10` is spoken as `"ONE ZERO"`, **never** as `"TEN"`.
   - Aisle `08` is spoken as `"ZERO EIGHT"`.
3. **Prefix Map Disambiguation**: In store locations, special prefix letters at index 0 indicate departments (e.g., `J` = `"JEWELLERY"`), which shares acoustic similarity with `"ZERO"`.

---

## 5. Evidence Rules (Decisions D3, D9, D11)

- **Decision D3 (Reference Example Badge)**:
  Authoritative seed observations from initial warehouse audits are labelled with a distinct **Reference example** badge (`seed_observation`). While historically documented, seed observations alone do not elevate uncorroborated candidates into Tier 1 without independent community approval.
- **Decision D9 (Reported Pairs Section)**:
  Section (a) lists reported confusion pairs for the active location. Directionality is isolated: reports where location $A$ was misrecognised as $B$ are displayed when viewing $A$.
- **Decision D11 (Acoustic Reason & Status Gate)**:
  Only reports with acoustic reasons (`similar_sounding` or `headset_misrecognition`) combined with status `approved` or `confirmed` (or provenance `moderator_confirmed` with status `community_submitted`) may elevate a candidate to Tier 1. Reports with reason `similar_check_character` or `other` appear in the reported list with the disclaimer *"Reported (layout / check-character), does not affect ranking"*, preventing non-acoustic submissions from skewing phonetic rankings. Disallowed statuses (`flagged`, `rejected`, `archived`, `pending`) never elevate.

---

## 6. Unsupported Inputs

The engine operates strictly on 4-character warehouse location codes conformant with Store (`[0-9]{2}[A-Z]{2}` or `[A-Z][0-9][A-Z]{2}`) and RFC (`[A-Z0-9+/-]{4}`) grammar. The following inputs are unsupported:
- Strings exceeding 4 characters (e.g., 5-character mezzanine codes, free-text strings).
- Non-grammar strings, punctuation, or wildcards (`???`, `*`, SQL injection tokens).
- Whitespace-only or empty strings.

---

## 7. Known Assumptions & Areas Needing Validation

1. **Independent Token Error Assumption**: The acoustic alignment assumes token substitutions occur independently. In reality, co-articulation, warehouse reverberation, and headset microphone clipping can cause compound acoustic shifts.
2. **Multi-Token Shift Seeds**: Certain warehouse seed observations (e.g., `08MN` / `20AN` and `81ZE` / `50ER`) involve 3 to 4 differing tokens. In the algorithmic model, these exceed the 2-token Tier 2/3 caps and appear under **Reported Pairs** or the **Arithmetic Pool**.
3. **Database Provenance Field**: In the current database schema, moderation workflows update report status to `'approved'` rather than modifying `provenance`. The `'moderator_confirmed'` provenance branch exists for future schema extensibility.

## 8. Benchmark Evaluation Suite

A dedicated, opt-in benchmark suite is provided to evaluate model predictions against historical observations.

### Benchmark Data Format (`benchmarks/voice_confusion_benchmark.jsonl`)
Lines in JSON Lines format:
```json
{"a":"08MN","b":"20AN","mode":"store","source":"seed_observation","confused":true,"count":12,"date":"2026-10-18"}
```

### Running the Benchmark
```bash
npm run benchmark:voice
```

### Future Evaluation Needs
- **Real Transcripts**: Collection of timestamped audio recordings and ASR decoder confidence scores from live stockroom environments.
- **Negative Controls**: Non-confused pairs with identical check characters, and acoustically close pairs with differing check characters.
- **Held-Out Test Sets**: Partitioning data into distinct training/tuning sets and held-out validation sets to avoid overfitting acoustic distance weights.
